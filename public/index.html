how this works

new runs one investigation: a simulated human learner asks your tutor one scoped question at a time until it can explain the idea in its own words.

full does that, then the tutor picks 3 more related concepts to investigate — a small tour of the topic.

🧑 free is the same thing with the simulated learner taken out: you ask the questions. Everything else is identical — the same tutor, the same style, answers split into parts, the glossary, highlights, 🔍 double-check, branch, quiz, exam, both exports. Two differences. A turn is one model call instead of two, since nobody has to think up the question. And the confidence — the one thing the simulated learner used to supply — is yours: a slider under the conversation, asked at the only moment it can honestly be answered, right after you have read the answer. It stamps that turn, so the sparkline and the tree map's dial are a real trace of how the conversation actually landed. The header's 🧑 free starts one on a tree you already have, building on the conversation you were reading. Either way, ⤵ branch still hands any answer to the simulated learner, if you would rather it did the digging on that one.

📚 ground it in your own material — the fold under the topic box: paste a passage (a textbook section, an article, your lecture notes) and it's new or full that grounds the whole tree in it — the learner's questions anchor to what the passage actually says, and the tutor grounds every answer in it, saying plainly when the passage itself is wrong or oversimplified. A 📚 source button appears in the conversation header on a grounded tree — reopen or correct the passage any time.

→ next (in the conversation header) is that same step on any tree, one press at a time: the tutor reviews everything covered so far and picks the one concept that best builds on it — a why/how/when angle, not another "what is" — then investigates it. Its one-line why stays on the new conversation.

🔬 look deeper — next to → next once a conversation is finished, but the opposite move: not a new concept, the SAME one again, much deeper. A fresh full investigation grows from it, seeded with everything already covered so it never repeats itself, with the simulated learner pushed to expert level and the tutor told to set its usual brevity aside — internals, edge cases, tradeoffs, the "why this way and not another," the kind of questions worth asking once the basics are solid.

🧭 gaps — for a topic you half-know: a short interview before the digging. The tutor asks a few questions one at a time — following what your answers reveal, probing a belief that sounds off, sampling what you didn't mention — and stops as soon as it can place you (or when you say that's enough — map me; “no idea” is a perfectly good answer). Then it maps what's solid, what's shaky, and what's missing, and the investigation starts at the biggest gap — pitched at your evident level, never re-explaining what you have down, straightening the shaky bits as they come up. The map and the transcript stay on the tree (🧭 baseline in the header reopens them).

✓ skip what you already know — the fold above gaps: a faster, cheaper alternative for when you don't need a real interview, you just want to say upfront what you've already got. List it, one per line, and it applies to new or full — no extra model call, no back-and-forth. The learner won't ask about it, the tutor won't re-explain it, and both stay focused on what's actually still open. Also reopens from 🧭 baseline in the header, same as a real gaps assessment.

⤵ branch under any tutor answer re-investigates that answer more deeply, growing the tree. Every turn is two real model calls (~5–7¢). loose threads (under the tree map) lists the words the learner flagged but never chased — tap one to branch straight into it, or its ✕ to dismiss one you don't care about (↩ brings them all back).

tutor style changes how the tutor answers — from highly technical to simple everyday words — and applies from the next turn, even mid-run.

answer length is the hard ceiling on one answer, in words — 150 by default, which is what this app ran on for its whole life. Pick a preset or type your own number (40–1600). The rest of the tutor's length brief scales with it, so raising the ceiling really does buy longer answers instead of leaving a sentence count arguing with it. It binds your own custom tutors too, and 🔍 double-check is told the same number so it never "corrects" an answer for being exactly as long as you asked. Below the default, the risk is a thin answer; above it, a lecture — the tutor is meant to answer the one question you asked, and a much longer answer is often several questions at once, which is what ⤵ branch and → next exist to do properly. At the top, spell it right out (1600 words) is for the ideas that only make sense whole: room for the mechanism, the why, an example and the caveats in a single answer. It's still one question answered, just with nothing left implicit — and the tutor is told to break a long answer into more parts, so you can still read it one idea at a time. The concise style keeps its own tighter limit whatever you set. Like the tutor style, it is remembered per profile.

learner sets how much the simulated learner already knows, from a curious novice to an expert from a neighbouring field — so its questions can keep up with the tutor. auto (the default) matches the tutor style; each investigation keeps the learner it started with.

answer parts — when an answer has distinct aspects (the why, an example, a caveat), each shows as its own labelled card under the direct answer. Tap a card to fold or unfold it — one idea at a time.

glossary — every word in it is one you put there. Nothing is added automatically: select a word or two in any answer and the chip's ➕ add files it (free — no model call), or tap the 🔍 word the learner flagged to add that one. It lives in the tree, so it syncs and exports with it. A definition is a separate step, whenever you want it: ✎ define it on the entry, or define N missing for the whole list at once. The chip's ✎ define is a plain lookup — it tells you what a word means and adds nothing; if the answer was worth keeping, the popover's ➕ add to glossary keeps it, definition and all. Dotted-underlined words in answers are defined glossary terms — tap one to read it right there. ⛏ dig is for when a definition isn't enough: the tutor is asked what that thing actually is in this context, and the answer lands in the conversation as your turn. ▶ investigate is dig's big sibling — it takes exactly what you selected (a word or a whole sentence) and starts a fresh investigation on it in its own tree, carrying nothing over from what you were reading, just as if you'd typed it into the topic box and pressed new. ✕ forget in the sidebar takes a term back out for good. anki cards downloads the defined terms as a file Anki imports directly.

🗣 explain it back — the Feynman loop. Explain a conversation in your own words, from memory, as if teaching a friend; the tutor reads it against what was actually covered and answers with what's solid, the one gap that matters, one question to push deeper — and an honest verdict: ✓ clean, ≈ close, or △ gappy. Ideas only, never the spelling. If it isn't clean, the box turns into answering that one question — shown right above the box, no scrolling to find it — instead of re-explaining everything, and Enter sends a short answer straight away (Shift+Enter for a line break; a fresh explanation still uses Ctrl/Cmd+Enter, since that box is a paragraph, not a one-liner). The tutor keeps pushing on the SAME nuance across replies, crediting what's already resolved — a ✓ clean round is praise, not a reset: the tutor still leaves a real next question on the table, and it never re-flags something you've already covered as missing. Keep going for as long as there's something worth chewing on, or just close the box; every round of that thread is right there on screen above the box (what you said, what it then asked), not just held invisibly — what the tutor is judging you against is exactly what you can see. Every reply is kept, the trail of verdicts shows the thread narrowing in, and tapping an old attempt brings its words back. ⤳ chase it sends the tutor's probing question into the conversation as your own turn instead, if you'd rather have it answered there. If the probe lands on something you just don't know, ❓ bank it parks that question in your global question bank and leaves the thread running — no model call, nothing interrupted; it reads ✓ banked once it's in. The 🔊 (or s) reads the feedback aloud. A clean explanation comes due again later — 3 days, then 7, then longer — and the 🗣 explain again nudge in the words tab (and ⌘K) takes you straight to what's due: re-explaining just before you'd forget is the strongest rehearsal there is. Your latest explanation of each conversation also heads into both exports, and 📊 progress tracks attempts, clean explanations, and what's due another pass.

🎓 quiz me — a few multiple-choice questions written from what this tree actually covered (your examples, your terms, your near-misses). Questions are written once and kept with the tree; retakes just reshuffle. The 🔊 beside the question (or the s key) reads it and each choice aloud in your chosen voice — and after you answer, the explanation too.

📄 exam — the quiz's harder sibling: a written paper on the conversation you have open. Essay questions (3–8 of them, 5 by default), one box each, one submit at the end. The examiner sets them without ever quoting the conversation, so nothing on the paper can be answered by remembering a sentence of it — expect to explain a mechanism, apply the idea to a case that never came up, and take apart a claim that sounds right. Each is out of 10, marked against the scheme it was set with, and what comes back per question is two paragraphs: what your answer earned, and what a full-mark answer would have said. Marks go to the ideas and the reasoning first, with credit for using the technical vocabulary correctly — never for name-dropping it, and never lost for explaining the right thing in plain words. Answers save as you type, so a paper survives closing the tab; an unfinished one waits in 📅 today. No idea on a question? ❓ bank it parks it in your global question bank and you carry on. 📊 progress keeps your papers, and both exports carry the marked script.

🗺 survey — for a broad topic, map before diving: the tutor breaks it into the foundations it's built on (each with a one-line why, two levels deep). investigate any piece to run a normal conversation on it — the first roots the tree, later ones build on what's covered — and ⤵ deeper breaks a piece down further, and know it marks a piece you already understand as covered without a conversation (tap ✓ known to unmark). The map lives with the tree and tracks coverage, so you can come back any time.

profiles — named interests (say, computer-science) that scope the whole app. ＋ above the tree list makes one: it starts empty and selected, and everything you do while it's selected files under it — conversations, flashcards, glossary, highlights, banked questions, the docket, the stats, the study sheet, even which custom tutors are on offer and the tutor style and learner level you read that interest with. Switch chips and the app is about that interest and nothing else; all shows everything. To file an existing conversation somewhere, use the this tree: picker under the list. ✎ renames a profile everywhere at once, ✕ deletes it and moves its conversations back to no profile — they are never deleted with it. A tree's profile travels and syncs with the tree; which profile you're in syncs too, so every device is in the same one.

🔁 review — every defined term in the active profile's trees is a flashcard. Recall it, flip, grade yourself (again / good / easy), and the card is scheduled further out each time — spaced repetition without leaving the app. The schedule syncs with the trees, so any device continues where you stopped. The 🔊 on a card (or the s key) says the term — and after the flip, the answer — in your chosen voice. The ⌨ type toggle turns the deck around: the definition asks and you type the term, checked leniently (case, punctuation and a single typo don't count against you) — a stricter test of recall than flipping and judging yourself. Fat-fingered a grade? ↩ (or the u key) takes the last one back and restores the card's schedule. The 🎧 drill toggle (or the d key) runs the deck hands-free: each term is spoken, a recall pause follows (set it with the ⏱ stepper), then the answer — and the card is marked good and the next begins. Press 1–3 at any moment to grade a card yourself instead; the summary says how many the drill marked on its own, and ↩ still takes the last one back.

★ highlights — select a longer stretch of an answer and the chip offers ★ highlight: a highlighter band that persists and syncs (tap a mark to lift it). The ★ highlights button in the words tab collects every marked passage across the profile's trees — tap one to jump back to it in context.

🏷 my words — the mirror of ✎ define. That one asks the tutor what a term means; this is you saying it, in brackets, right there in the sentence. Select a word, type your own explanation, press Enter, and "so if a plant mitochondria is active" reads "so if a plant mitochondria (power house of the cell) is active" from then on. Shift+T does it without reaching for the chip — select, press, type. No model call, no cost. Your words are tinted so they can never be mistaken later for something the tutor said, and the brackets are real text, so copying the block copies them too. Tap your own words to reword them — an empty box takes them back out. They travel with the tree, sync, and are spliced into the sentence in both exports, exactly where you put them.

⚡ facts — the breadth mode, next to new/full/survey. Everything else here ends in a conversation: slow, scoped, one idea at a time. This is one call that returns the landscape — a few dozen one-sentence facts, grouped, scannable in a couple of minutes — so what you dig into next is chosen rather than stumbled into. Facts are badged by kind (mechanism, number, edge, and misconception, which flags a common belief that's wrong — the most useful kind on the list). Filter them by typing; spelling is forgiven as everywhere else. Then take an exit: ▶ turns a fact into a full investigation in that tree, ❓ banks it as a question for whenever. The list stays on the tree, tracks which facts you chased, syncs, and heads both exports.

🖼 image — select the descriptive sentence you'd like to see, and the chip's 🖼 image draws a figure of it, right under that answer. Nothing is ever illustrated on its own: the sentence you point at is the whole brief, which is what keeps the picture about the thing you were reading. A tutor reads the passage first and decides what actually has a shape worth drawing — and sometimes answers that nothing here does, which is a real answer, not a failure. Under each figure: 🔍 explain has the tutor walk through it part by part, 💬 ask puts your own question about it (both land in the conversation as your turn), ↻ redraw tries again — say what it should show instead, or leave the box blank — and ✕ removes it. Figures travel in the tree, sync, and go into export html; the markdown export carries their descriptions.

📅 today — one place for everything waiting across the active profile: cards due, conversations due another explanation, loose threads from every tree (not just the open one — chase one, or ✕ the ones you don't care about), and survey maps with uncovered ground. Tap anything to jump straight to it. The button appears in the words tab whenever something's waiting.

search — the find tab looks through every tree: the conversations, the glossary, your highlights and your notes. Spelling is forgiven: if nothing matches exactly, close spellings count — one typo or a swapped pair of letters per word, in any word order — and the results say so. The glossary filter and Ctrl+K forgive the same way.

custom tutors (the ✎ next to the style picker) let you write your own: name it, describe how it should answer, and it syncs to your other devices. The base rules always stay in force.

ask the tutor yourself — the box under any conversation sends your own question with the same context. It's recorded as your turn (🧑), and the simulated learner never sees it.

💬 ask about a passage — select any sentence in an answer and the chip's 💬 ask opens a bar with that passage quoted above it. Type your question and it goes to the tutor with the passage, so the reply is about that exact claim rather than the topic at large. It's saved as your turn (🧑) with the passage above it, in the transcript and in both exports. ⛏ dig is the same idea with the question already written for you.

📝 my notes (Shift+N) — your own synthesis of a tree, and it formats as you type: type ## and the line becomes a heading, close a ** and the word goes bold. The markers stay put, just dimmed, so nothing shifts under your cursor. The toolbar does headings, bullets, numbered lists, quotes, bold, italic, underline and code (Ctrl+B/I/U/H, Ctrl+Z to undo). It saves as you write — and again whenever the tab is hidden or closed — then travels with the tree and heads every export.

❓ question banks — jot down a question without losing your place: no dialog, no scrolling away, just a small bar to type it into. There are two, answered completely differently. Press q anywhere (no tree needed) for the global bank — investigate one later and it starts a brand-new investigation with its own text as the topic, exactly as if you'd typed it into the topic box and pressed new; the ❓ global questions row in the words tab opens it. Press Shift+Q while reading for the local bank — saved against the passage you were reading, it stays inside that same conversation: investigate asks the tutor directly on the spot, or run all queues every pending one in a pass, exactly like "full" queues its follow-ups. The ❓ questions button in the conversation header opens the local bank for that tree. In the global bank, ✨ suggest questions reads what you've banked and proposes a few it implies but you never wrote down — the prerequisite underneath them, the obvious next step, the case they all assume. They're proposals: nothing joins the bank until you press + on that one, and ✕ throws it away. Either bank, once two are waiting, offers ⇅ order by dependency: it sorts what's waiting into the order it's best learned in — where two questions touch the same idea, whichever the other one needs answered first goes first. Either bank folds answered questions under a disclosure with a jump back to what they became. A local question can change its mind: ↗ promote moves it to the global bank, for when something you jotted mid-read turns out not to be about that passage at all but a topic in its own right. It stops being one question to this tutor and becomes its own investigation, keeping the profile of the tree it came from; the local bank folds it away under ↗ moved to the global bank so you can still see where it went.

Trees live in this browser. Use save .know.json to move one to another device or the CLI — the file format is identical. On a phone or tablet, saving opens the share sheet (AirDrop, Save to Files, Drive…) instead of a download. Coming back the other way, drop the file anywhere on the page (or use import).

📜 study sheet — one revision document for the whole profile: each tree's notes, your best explanation of each conversation (with its verdict), the passages you highlighted, and every defined term merged into one alphabetical glossary at the end. The transcripts stay in the per-tree exports — this is the sheet you'd revise from before an exam.
